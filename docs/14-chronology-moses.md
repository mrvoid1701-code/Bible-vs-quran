# Chronology 5 — Moses and the Exodus

Moses (Musa) is the **most-mentioned figure in the Qur'an**; the parallel
with the Bible is the closest in the whole storyline — which makes the
divergences especially diagnostic.

## Shared narrative

Birth under threat and the basket on the river; flight to Midian; the
burning bush/fire and commission; the staff-to-serpent and the "white
hand" signs; Aaron given as spokesman for the slow-of-speech Moses;
confrontation with Pharaoh; plagues; deliverance through the sea and
Pharaoh's destruction; revelation at the mountain; the people's rebellion
(the calf). The agreement extends even to **specific details** (the
basket, Midian, the staff, Aaron the helper) — the tightest parallel in
the storyline.

## What is said at the theophany — a revealing divergence

Both record the bush/fire encounter; **what God says about Himself
differs, and the difference is diagnostic** (it is the
[cap. 09](09-nature-of-god.md) seam in miniature):

- **Bible:** "**I AM WHO I AM** (*ehyeh asher ehyeh*)… say *YHWH*… this is
  My name forever" (Exod 3:14–15) — self-disclosure as the **covenant
  name**, relational and personal.
- **Qurʾan:** "Indeed **I am Allah; there is no deity except Me, so
  worship Me**" (Q 20:14; cf. 27:9; 28:30) — self-disclosure as the
  **declaration of *tawḥīd*** and a command to worship.

Same scene, same prophet — but Exodus discloses a **name**, the Qurʾan
discloses a **doctrine**. This is the nature-of-God contrast (covenantal
self-naming vs. transcendent unity-assertion) surfacing at the very first
divine speech to Moses.

## The key divergences (text)

### 1. The golden calf — who is guilty?

- **Bible:** **Aaron himself makes the calf** (Exod 32:2–4) — and the
  Bible does **not** whitewash it: "the LORD was angry enough with Aaron
  to destroy him" (Deut 9:20), yet Aaron still becomes high priest. This
  is the **criterion of embarrassment** again (cf.
  [cap. 15](15-chronology-david-prophets.md)): an unflattering detail kept
  about a hero.
- **Qurʾan:** the calf is made by **al-Sāmirī**; **Hārūn is explicitly
  exonerated** — he warns the people and is innocent (Q 20:85–94),
  protecting **prophetic impeccability (*ʿiṣma*)**.
- *Symmetry note:* critics raise an **anachronism objection to
  "al-Sāmirī"** (linking it to the post-exilic *Samaritans*, centuries
  later) — **structurally the same kind of objection as Haman below**.
  Muslim scholars reply that *Sāmirī* is a personal/tribal name, not the
  later Samaritans. Like Haman: a **real but contested** point, recorded
  as **unresolved**, and noted on **both** sides for symmetry.

### 2. Pharaoh's end

- **Qur'an-only details:** Pharaoh's **deathbed profession of faith is
  rejected** (Q 10:90–91) and "**We will preserve your body as a sign**"
  (Q 10:92). This has no biblical parallel; modern apologetic use of
  Egyptian mummies is **rhetoric, not proof** (Methodology §1.4).

### 3. The "Haman" question

- The Qurʾan places a **Hāmān** in **Pharaoh's Egypt** as his minister,
  ordered to build a tower (Q 28:6, 38; 40:36–37).
- The only biblical Haman is a **Persian** official in **Esther**, ~a
  millennium later and a different empire — the standard **anachronism
  objection**.
- **Muslim responses (stated fully):** "Hāmān" may be a **common name** or
  a **title/office**; some apologists link it to the Egyptian priestly
  designation *ḥm-nṯr* ("servant of the god") — though that etymology is
  **itself rejected by most Egyptologists** and should not be oversold.
- **Verdict on this point:** the *prima facie* reading favours the
  anachronism charge, but it is **not a knockout** (Methodology §4); paired
  with the symmetric al-Sāmirī objection, the honest record is
  **contested, not decisive on either side**.

### 4. Dramatic elements unique to each

- **Qurʾan-only:** the **magicians believe and are martyred** by Pharaoh
  ("we will not prefer you over the clear proofs", Q 20:70–73; 7:120–126)
  — a vivid faith-under-persecution motif; in the Bible the magicians
  merely fail and concede "this is the finger of God" (Exod 8:19).
  **Qārūn** (Korah), swallowed with his wealth, is moralised as the
  archetype of arrogant riches (Q 28:76–82).
- These are **theological dramatisations**, not corruption proofs —
  each text shaping the shared events to its own emphasis.

## Arbiter verdict — Moses

**Present.** The richest shared narrative in the storyline; divergences
concentrated on **moral roles** and **a few Qur'an-only specifics**.

**Weigh.**
- *Systematic, theology-driven:* Aaron's exoneration is **internally
  consistent** with *ʿiṣma* — a doctrine, not a copying slip; the Bible's
  retention of Aaron's guilt is the **criterion of embarrassment**.
- *Self-disclosure divergence:* "I AM / YHWH" (a **name**) vs. "I am
  Allah, no deity but Me" (a **doctrine**) — the cap. 09 seam, textual and
  clean.
- *Symmetric contested objections:* **Hāmān** (anachronism vs. Egypt)
  **and** **al-Sāmirī** (anachronism vs. the Samaritans) are the **same
  kind** of objection — each *prima facie* favours the critic, each has a
  non-trivial rebuttal, **neither is decisive**. They **offset**.
- *Out of remit:* mummy/concordist arguments and the *ḥm-nṯr* etymology —
  rhetoric/disputed, set aside. The **external historicity** of a large
  Exodus (debated by archaeologists) pressures **both** texts equally.

**Judge.** On the **shared core**: the **strongest draw in the whole
storyline** — agreement down to specifics. On **theophany self-
disclosure**: a clean **divergence** tracking cap. 09 (name vs. doctrine),
non-arbitrable as to which is "right." On **the calf**: a **doctrine-
driven divergence** (*ʿiṣma* vs. embarrassment), each internally coherent.
On **Hāmān / al-Sāmirī**: **mutually contested and offsetting** — the
dossier scores **neither** as a win (Methodology §4). The seam reappears
exactly where Islamic prophetology requires sinless prophets — setting up
the next chapter.
