# Introduction: The Arbiter's Framing

## Purpose

This project is a **comparative religious-studies dossier** examining two
questions that dominate Christian–Muslim apologetic exchange:

1. **The historical / textual question** — How reliably has each scripture
   (the Bible and the Qur'an) been transmitted, and how strong are the
   mutual accusations of *corruption*?
2. **The theological / logical question** — How do strict monotheism
   (Islamic *tawhid*; the Jewish *Shema*) and Trinitarian monotheism
   compare in internal coherence and in fidelity to their own source texts?

## The arbiter stance

This dossier takes **no side**. It is written as a referee, not an
advocate. That means:

- **No confessional assumptions.** Neither "the Bible is the inerrant Word
  of God" nor "the Qur'an is the uncorrupted final revelation" is taken as
  a premise. Both are claims to be examined, not axioms.
- **Symmetry of scrutiny.** Every standard applied to one scripture is
  applied to the other. An argument that damages the Bible's transmission
  is tested against the Qur'an's transmission, and vice versa.
- **Mainstream scholarship first.** The baseline is peer-reviewed academic
  consensus in textual criticism, manuscript studies, and the history of
  doctrine — not the strongest claim either apologetic camp can make.
- **Transparency about uncertainty.** Where the evidence is genuinely
  contested or thin, that is stated plainly rather than resolved by
  rhetoric.

## How each topic is concluded

By request, each major section ends with a three-part verdict:

1. **Present** — both sides' best case, fairly stated.
2. **Weigh** — where the evidence is strong, weak, or contested for each
   side.
3. **Judge** — where (and only where) the evidence warrants it, the
   stronger position is named, with explicit caveats. Where the evidence
   cannot decide, that is the verdict.

## What this dossier is *not*

- It is **not** a faith statement or a conversion tool.
- It does **not** rule on whether God exists or which religion is "true."
  Those are not historical or textual questions and lie outside an
  arbiter's competence.
- It does **not** treat apologetic debate performance as evidence. A
  debater "winning" a stage event is rhetoric, not data.

## Scope

| Covered | Not covered |
|---|---|
| Manuscript & textual transmission of both scriptures | Comparative ethics / law |
| The Islamic doctrine of *tahrif* (corruption) | Eschatology in detail |
| Trinity vs. strict monotheism (textual + logical) | Mysticism / spirituality |
| Key academic and apologetic voices on both sides | Political history of either community |
