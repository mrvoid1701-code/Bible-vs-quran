# Chronology 1 — Creation

**Part III** walks the shared storyline from creation to the end. At each
stage: the Bible's text, the Qur'an's text, where they converge, where
they collide, then a present/weigh/judge verdict. The recurring finding
will be that **one continuous theological seam** — God's nature → sin →
forgiveness → Christ → the end — runs through every episode.

---

## The Bible's text

- **Genesis 1:** creation in **six days**, ordered — light (1); sky/
  firmament (2); land, sea, plants (3); sun, moon, stars (4); fish, birds
  (5); land animals and humankind, male and female (6); God **rests** (7),
  hallowing the Sabbath (Gen 2:2–3).
- **Genesis 2:** a second, differently-ordered account — the man formed
  **first** from dust, then the garden, then animals, then woman from his
  side.
- Creation is by divine speech ("And God said… and it was so"); the NT
  reads this as creation **from nothing** (Heb 11:3; John 1:3; Col 1:16).

## The Qur'an's text

- Creation in **six "days"** (*ayyam*, periods): Q 7:54; 10:3; 25:59.
- By sheer command: "**Be — and it is**" (*kun fayakun*, Q 2:117; 36:82).
- Heavens and earth were **a joined mass, then parted** (Q 21:30); the
  heaven "expanded" (Q 51:47).
- Humankind from **clay / dust / a drop** (Q 15:26; 22:5; 32:7–9); Adam
  taught "**the names**" (Q 2:31); the angels prostrate to Adam, Iblis
  refuses (Q 2:34).
- A breakdown in Q 41:9–12 lists earth in 2 + sustenance in 4 + heavens in
  2 — read additively this looks like **eight**, the classic *6-vs-8*
  exegetical crux (resolved by traditional exegetes as overlapping, not
  additive, periods).

## Convergence

Six creative periods; a single God creating by **fiat**, with **no rival
deity** and **no pre-existent co-creator**; humankind formed from **earth/
clay**; a primordial couple; a tempter figure.

## Collision

1. **Internal Genesis tension:** Gen 1 (plants day 3, *before* the sun day
   4; humans last) vs. Gen 2 (man *before* plants and animals) — a
   well-known source-critical seam in the Bible itself.
2. **The Qur'an's 6-vs-8 arithmetic** of Q 41:9–12 — an internal
   exegetical knot, paralleling Genesis's internal knot.
3. **Does God rest?** Gen 2:2–3 has God **resting** on the seventh day.
   Q 50:38 states creation occurred "**and no fatigue touched Us**" —
   which reads naturally as a **deliberate correction of Genesis**. This
   is a genuine theological divergence, not a copying detail: it flows
   straight from the [nature-of-God](09-nature-of-god.md) contrast
   (absolute transcendence vs. an anthropomorphic-friendly text).

## The creation of the human being — and "Let Us make man"

### What God says and does

- **Gen 1:26–28:** "**Let Us make man** in **Our** image (*ṣelem*), after
  **Our** likeness (*demut*)" → then v.27 immediately shifts to the
  **singular**: "So God created man in **His** image… male and female He
  created them"; v.28 the blessing and mandate (be fruitful, fill, subdue,
  have dominion).
- **Gen 2:7:** God **forms** man from dust and **breathes** the breath of
  life; 2:18 "not good that the man should be alone"; the woman from his
  side (2:21–23).
- The same plural recurs: Gen 3:22 ("become like **one of Us**") and Gen
  11:7 ("**let Us** go down").

### The plural "Us" — read logically, all options on the table

1. **Divine council** — God addressing the heavenly host (cf. Job 38:7;
   1 Kgs 22:19; Isa 6:8). A mainstream academic reading; difficulty: man
   bears *God's* image, not the angels'.
2. **Plural of self-deliberation** — a rhetorical cohortative, God taking
   counsel with Himself.
3. **Plural of majesty** ("royal We") — popular, but **linguistically
   contested**: a majestic plural on the verb is not clearly attested this
   way in Biblical Hebrew.
4. **Trinitarian reading** — intra-divine plurality, supported only
   **retrospectively** by the NT (John 1:3; Col 1:16; the Spirit in Gen
   1:2).

Textual note: **Elohim** is grammatically a plural form but governs a
**singular** verb (*baraʾ*) — understood as intensive/majestic naming,
**not** polytheism. The v.26-plural → v.27-singular shift is the crux.

### The Qur'an's "We" — and the symmetry test

The Qur'an pervasively has God say "**We created man**" (Q 15:26;
23:12–14; 32:7–9), alternating with "I" in the Adam scene (Q 2:30 "**I**
am placing a *khalifa*"; Q 15:29 / 38:72 "I breathed into him of **My
spirit**"). In Islamic doctrine this "We" is **unambiguously the plural of
majesty** (*taʿẓim*), explicitly **not** plurality of persons (*tawhid*).

**The decisive symmetry point:** if the Qur'an's "We" is accepted as a
majestic/non-personal plural, then **that same option is fully available
for Genesis's "Us."** By the dossier's symmetry rule (Methodology §3),
"Let Us make man" is therefore **suggestive only within the cumulative
case** of [cap. 05](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md) — **not a stand-alone
proof** of the Trinity.

### The real divergence about the human: *imago Dei* vs. *khalifa*

- **Bible:** humankind in the **image of God** (*imago Dei*) — maximal
  dignity, representation of God on earth.
- **Qur'an:** **no "image of God"**; instead *khalifa* (vicegerent), an
  innate upright nature (*fiṭra*), and God's breathed spirit. *(A ḥadith
  "Adam in His image" exists, but it is **tradition, not Qurʾan**, and its
  pronoun referent is disputed among scholars — flagged as tradition.)*

This is the substantive seam at the creation of humanity, and it feeds
directly into [cap. 11 — the Fall](11-chronology-fall.md).

## Arbiter verdict — creation

**Present.** Both: one God, six periods, creation by command, man from
earth. Bible adds Sabbath rest and a two-account structure; Qur'an adds
"join-then-part" and the *kun fayakun* formula.

**Weigh.**
- *Symmetric weakness:* each text carries an **internal ordering/number
  puzzle** (Genesis's two accounts; the Qur'an's 6-vs-8). Neither can use
  it against the other without conceding its own (Methodology §3).
- *Real divergence:* the **"God rests" vs. "no fatigue"** point is
  substantive and textual, and Q 50:38 looks corrective by design.
- *Interpretively open:* "**Let Us make man**" admits four serious
  readings; the Trinitarian one is **retrospective**, and the Qur'an's
  majestic "We" proves a non-personal plural is natural — so by symmetry
  it is **not a stand-alone Trinity proof**.
- *Substantive:* **imago Dei vs. khalifa/fiṭra** is the real divergence
  about what the human *is*.
- *Out of remit:* concordance with modern cosmology/biology is argued
  apologetically on **both** sides; the arbiter does not rule on science-
  vs-text harmonization (Methodology §5).

**Judge.** On the **shared frame**: a draw — strikingly similar. On the
**internal puzzles**: a draw — both have one. On **"does God rest"**: a
**clean divergence**, with the Qur'an's wording functioning as an explicit
revision of Genesis; which portrayal is "better" depends on the prior
theology-of-God premise already shown to be non-arbitrable
([cap. 09](09-nature-of-god.md)). On **"Let Us make man"**: by the
even-handed symmetry rule it is **suggestive, not probative** — the
Qur'an's majestic "We" shows the plural alone settles nothing. On the
**human being itself**: *imago Dei* vs. *khalifa/fiṭra* is a **genuine
divergence**, internally coherent on each side, and it is what carries the
seam into the Fall. The seam appears immediately.
