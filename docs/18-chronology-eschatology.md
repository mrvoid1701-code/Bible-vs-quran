# Chronology 9 — Eschatology: The End

The storyline closes where it began — with the seam intact.

## Striking agreement

Both texts affirm: a coming end of the age; **bodily resurrection of the
dead**; a universal **Day of Judgment**; **heaven and hell** as real
destinations; the **return of Jesus**; a great **deceiver** figure; and
**final, perfect justice**.

> Precision (Methodology §1): the **Dajjal** is principally **hadith**,
> not Qur'an; the **Antichrist** is principally the **Johannine epistles**
> and tradition, lightly in the NT. Both "deceiver" motifs are flagged as
> **more traditional than scriptural**, on each side equally.

## The decisive divergences

### 1. The criterion of salvation — the seam's terminus
- **Islam:** judgment by **faith + deeds**, under God's sovereign
  **mercy/will**; **no atonement** is needed (direct downstream of
  [cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md) and [cap. 09 §2.4](09-nature-of-god.md)).
- **Christianity:** salvation by **grace through Christ's atoning work**,
  received by faith — because the [cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md) Fall
  created a debt only the [cap. 16](16-chronology-jesus.md) cross pays.

The **same fork from the garden** determines the **gate of paradise**.
This is the seam's endpoint: one continuous logical thread from creation
to judgment.

### 2. Jesus' role at the end
- **Islam:** returns as a **Muslim**, breaks the cross, defeats the
  Dajjal, affirms Islam, then dies.
- **Christianity:** returns as the **divine Judge and King**.
- Same figure, **mutually exclusive roles** — the
  [cap. 05](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md)/[16](16-chronology-jesus.md)
  divergence projected onto the last day.

### 3. Mediation and the picture of paradise
- **Mediation:** Islam — limited **intercession** (*shafa'a*) by God's
  leave; Christianity — **Christ the one mediator** (1 Tim 2:5).
- **Paradise:** the Qur'an's imagery is vividly **physical** (gardens,
  rivers, companions); the NT emphasis is more **theocentric** (to "see
  God", Rev 22:4) though it **also** has new-creation and feast imagery.
  Presented **without caricature** on either side.

## Arbiter verdict — the end

**Present.** A **large shared eschatological frame**; decisive divergence
on the **mechanism of salvation** and **Christ's identity/role**.

**Weigh.**
- *Strong shared structure:* resurrection, judgment, heaven/hell,
  returning Christ, deceiver, final justice — substantive, not vague.
- *Decisive divergence:* the salvation criterion is **not** an emphasis
  difference; it is the **logical terminus of the [cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md)
  fork** and cannot be true both ways.
- *Symmetric flags:* the deceiver motifs are **traditional more than
  scriptural** on **both** sides.

**Judge.** On the **eschatological scaffolding**: a strong **draw** —
notably similar. On the **gate of salvation** and **Christ at the end**:
**not arbitrable** — each system is **internally coherent**, and which is
**true** is a theological-truth question the arbiter has, from the start,
declined ([Methodology §5](01-methodology.md)).

## The whole arc — one seam, restated

| Stage | The single dividing question |
|---|---|
| [God's nature](09-nature-of-god.md) | Simple/transcendent, or triune/incarnational love? |
| [Creation](10-chronology-creation.md) | Does God "rest" / enter creaturely terms? |
| [The Fall](11-chronology-fall.md) | Is sin inherited (→ redeemer needed)? |
| [Flood](12-chronology-flood.md) / [Abraham](13-chronology-abraham.md) | Salvation by lineage, or by personal faith? |
| [Moses](14-chronology-moses.md) / [David](15-chronology-david-prophets.md) | Are prophets fallible, or impeccable? |
| [Jesus](16-chronology-jesus.md) | Did the cross happen, and what is Jesus? |
| [Muhammad](17-chronology-muhammad.md) | Is revelation sealed, and is he foretold? |
| The End | Saved by atonement, or by deeds under mercy? |

**Final word of Part III.** The two scriptures share a single skeleton and
divide along **one continuous, traceable seam**, set at the Fall and
running unbroken to the Judgment. On the **bare historical questions**
(crucifixion as event; manuscript-based prophecy claims) the neutral
method **does lean**, and it leans by the **same rule applied to both
sides**. On the **theological questions** (Trinity, atonement, finality,
who is saved) the arbiter's consistent verdict is the honest one:
**internally coherent on each side, and not decidable by evidence or logic
alone** — which is exactly what neutrality, held to the end, looks like.
