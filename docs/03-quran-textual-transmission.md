# The Qur'an: Textual Transmission and Historical Evidence

The same yardstick from [Methodology](01-methodology.md) is now applied to
the Qur'an. The traditional account and the manuscript evidence are
presented separately, then compared.

---

## Part A — The traditional account

- The Qur'an was revealed to Muhammad over c. 610–632 CE and memorized and
  written on scattered materials.
- Under the caliph **Abu Bakr** a collection was made (motivated, the
  reports say, by the death of memorizers at the Battle of Yamama).
- Under the caliph **ʿUthman** (c. 650 CE) a standardized consonantal
  *rasm* was promulgated and **divergent codices were ordered destroyed**
  (e.g., the codices associated with Ibn Masʿud and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb).
- The text was revealed, tradition holds, in **seven *ahruf*** (modes);
  the canonical ***qiraʾat*** (readings) are the surviving regulated
  recitation traditions.

This account is internally coherent but is itself a *tradition about
history* (Methodology §1.3) — its reliability is tested against physical
evidence, not assumed.

---

## Part B — The manuscript evidence

- **Birmingham folios (Mingana 1572a):** parchment radiocarbon-dated to a
  very early range (with high probability before c. 645–660 CE). Its text
  is essentially the **standard ʿUthmanic** text — strong evidence that the
  standard consonantal skeleton was fixed **very early**.
- **Sanaa palimpsest (DAM 01-27.1):** the most important find. The
  *upper* text is standard ʿUthmanic. The **erased *lower* text** is a
  **genuinely different, non-ʿUthmanic recension** — a "companion codex"
  type. Studied by Behnam Sadeghi, Mohsen Goudarzi, and Asma Hilali, it
  shows **real textual variants**: different words, word order,
  pronouns, and occasionally different formulae — precisely the kind of
  pre-standardization plurality the tradition itself describes (the
  burned codices).
- **Topkapi, Samarkand, Tübingen, Codex Parisino-petropolitanus** and
  other early *hijazi* manuscripts: broadly confirm an early, stable
  consonantal *rasm* with orthographic development (dotting/vowelling
  added over time).

### The reading traditions (*qiraʾat*)

- The undotted early *rasm* could be vocalized differently. Ten *qiraʾat*
  became canonical. Today the world overwhelmingly uses **Ḥafṣ ʿan
  ʿAṣim**; **Warsh ʿan Nafiʿ** is used across much of North/West Africa.
- Differences between, e.g., Ḥafṣ and Warsh are mostly **vowels,
  pronunciation, and minor wording**; a smaller number touch sense.
  Apologetically these are usually presented as a *controlled,
  transmitted* phenomenon — analogous in scale, defenders argue, to minor
  NT variants, not to wholesale change.

### Academic positions

- **Mainstream (F. Déroche, B. Sadeghi, N. Sinai, A. Neuwirth, M.
  Cook):** the ʿUthmanic consonantal text was standardized **remarkably
  early** (within ~2 decades of Muhammad's death); the Qur'an is best read
  as a genuine **late-antique** text of the early-mid 7th c. The Sanaa
  lower text proves early variation existed but was suppressed by
  standardization.
- **Revisionists (J. Wansbrough, P. Crone, M. Cook's early work):**
  argued for much later canonization (8th–9th c.). The early manuscript
  datings (Birmingham, Sanaa) have **substantially undercut the strong
  revisionist position**, which is now a minority view.
- **Keith Small** (*Textual Criticism and Qurʾan Manuscripts*): the
  ʿUthmanic standardization achieved textual *unity* by **elimination of
  variants** (destroying codices), whereas NT unity is reconstructed by
  *comparison* of surviving variants — two different models of
  "preservation."

---

## Arbiter verdict — Qur'an transmission

**Present.**
- *Defense:* an exceptionally early-fixed consonantal *rasm* (Birmingham),
  a strong oral-memorization tradition, and a globally near-uniform text
  today.
- *Prosecution:* the standard text was achieved by **destroying rival
  codices** (so dissenting evidence was deliberately removed); the **Sanaa
  lower text** physically proves a different early recension existed; and
  the *qiraʾat* show the tradition is not, in fact, a single monolithic
  reading.

**Weigh.**
- "The Qur'an's consonantal skeleton was standardized very early" —
  **well supported** by manuscripts; this is the Qur'an's genuine
  evidentiary strength.
- "Therefore the Qur'an has been *perfectly* preserved with **no**
  variants" — **not supported**: Sanaa and the *qiraʾat* refute the
  strong form of that claim.
- The ʿUthmanic model trades **early uniformity** for **loss of the
  comparative evidence base** — we cannot fully reconstruct the
  pre-ʿUthmanic plurality precisely because it was destroyed.

**Judge.**
On the narrow historical question — *was the standard text fixed early?* —
the evidence favors the **Qur'an's defenders**: standardization was
impressively rapid and the resulting consonantal text is stable across the
early manuscripts. On the absolute claim — *the Qur'an has reached us with
literally zero textual variation* — the evidence favors the **critics**:
the Sanaa palimpsest and the canonical *qiraʾat* are physical, internal
counter-evidence. The defensible position is therefore: **early and stable,
but not variant-free**, and "preserved by elimination" is a different
historical claim from "preserved by abundant attestation."
