# Monotheism vs. the Trinity: Textual and Logical Analysis

Two questions are kept strictly separate (Methodology §5):

- **(a) Textual:** Which view does each scripture's *own* text better
  support?
- **(b) Logical:** Is the Trinity internally coherent, or a contradiction?

The arbiter does **not** rule on which is divinely true.

---

## 1. The two models defined

- **Strict (unitarian) monotheism** — God is one in person and essence.
  - Jewish *Shema*: "Hear O Israel, the LORD our God, the LORD is one"
    (Deut 6:4).
  - Islamic *tawhid*: Q 112 ("Say: He is God, One"); Q 4:171, 5:73, 5:116
    explicitly reject "three" and reject Jesus-as-God; associating
    partners (*shirk*) is the gravest sin.
- **Trinitarian monotheism** — one God in essence (*ousia*) eternally
  existing as three persons (*hypostaseis*): Father, Son, Spirit; the Son
  incarnate in Jesus. This is **not** tritheism (three gods) and **not**
  modalism (one person in three modes) — both were declared heresies.

---

## 2. The textual question — what do the sources say?

### Qur'an
Unambiguous and explicit for strict monotheism. The Qur'an *directly*
addresses and rejects the Trinity and the deity of Christ (Q 4:171,
5:72–75, 5:116). On the *text's own terms* there is no internal tension:
strict monotheism is stated outright.

> Note: Q 5:116 frames a "Trinity" as **God, Jesus, and Mary**. Scholars
> dispute whether this (i) misstates orthodox Trinitarianism, (ii) targets
> a real heterodox/Marian sect, or (iii) is rhetorical. This is itself a
> contested data point and is flagged, not resolved.

### Bible
There is **no single verse** that states the doctrine of the Trinity in
creedal form (and the clearest would-be proof-text, the **Comma Johanneum
1 John 5:7**, is **not original** — see
[Bible Transmission](02-bible-textual-transmission.md)). The Trinitarian
case is built *cumulatively*:

- **Strict-monotheism / unitarian-leaning data:** Deut 6:4; Isa 45:5;
  Mark 12:29 (Jesus affirms the *Shema*); John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6; "the
  Father is greater than I" (John 14:28); Jesus prays to God; 1 Tim 2:5.
- **High-Christology / Trinitarian data:** John 1:1, 1:14; John 8:58
  ("before Abraham was, I am"); John 10:30; John 20:28 ("My Lord and my
  God"); Phil 2:6–11; Col 1:15–20, 2:9; Heb 1; Matt 28:19 (triadic
  baptismal formula); 2 Cor 13:14; the Spirit as personal and divine
  (Acts 5:3–4).

So the **biblical text is genuinely bivalent**: it contains both strongly
monotheistic *and* strongly high-Christological strands. The Trinity is a
**later doctrinal synthesis** that attempts to hold *both* strands
together without collapsing into tritheism or denying Jesus' deity.

### Historical development (the academic record)

- Devotion to Jesus as in some sense divine is **very early** — Hurtado
  (*Lord Jesus Christ*) and Bauckham (*Jesus and the God of Israel*) argue
  for a high "Christology of divine identity" within the first two
  decades, *inside* a Jewish monotheistic frame.
- Ehrman (*How Jesus Became God*) argues the *exalted* status escalated
  over time from a lower starting point.
- The **formal** doctrine was hammered out against rival models: Arianism
  (Son created/subordinate) → **Nicaea 325** (*homoousios*, "same
  essence") → **Constantinople 381** (full deity of the Spirit) →
  **Chalcedon 451** (two natures of Christ). These councils *defined and
  regulated* the doctrine; the genetic fallacy (Methodology §4) forbids
  inferring from "defined in 325" that "the underlying belief was absent
  in 30–100" — that has to be argued from the texts, where the evidence is
  **mixed and early-contested**.

## 3. The logical question — is the Trinity coherent?

### The Islamic / unitarian objection
- It looks like a contradiction: God is one *and* three.
- It looks like *shirk*: associating a created/human being with God.
- Incarnation seems to impose creaturely limits (hunger, death, ignorance
  — Mark 13:32) on the immutable, transcendent God.

### The Trinitarian reply
- The classic formula avoids formal contradiction by **qualifying the
  terms**: God is one *in essence* and three *in person* — "one" and
  "three" are predicated of **different things**, so it is not "1=3."
- Models: **Latin/Augustinian** (one divine substance, three subsistent
  relations), **Social Trinitarianism** (three centers of consciousness in
  perfect unity of essence/will), **relative-identity** approaches
  (Geach/van Inwagen). Philosophers (W. L. Craig, R. Swinburne, P. van
  Inwagen, B. Leftow) defend coherence; critics (and many Muslim
  philosophers, classically the *kalam* tradition) argue every model
  either slides into **tritheism** or **modalism**.
- All analogies (water states, sun/light/heat, etc.) are admitted by
  careful theologians to be **imperfect** and are not the doctrine itself.

### Arbiter note on the logic
- "Triune" is **not a strict logical contradiction** *as stated*, because
  the predicates are indexed to different categories (essence vs. person).
  A bare "1 = 3" objection **fails**.
- However, the doctrine is **not transparently self-evident** either: its
  coherence depends on technical metaphysical machinery (essence/person,
  perichoresis, the *homoousion*) that is **not given in the texts** and
  is itself **internally contested among Trinitarian philosophers**.
- Strict monotheism (*tawhid* / unitarianism) has the advantage of
  **logical simplicity and immediacy**: it requires no special apparatus
  to state without contradiction.

---

## Arbiter verdict — monotheism vs. Trinity

**Present.**
- *Islamic/unitarian:* the Qur'an states strict monotheism outright; the
  Bible's clearest Trinity proof-text is a spurious insertion; the Trinity
  is a post-biblical synthesis and is logically strained.
- *Christian:* the NT contains undeniable high-Christological and triadic
  data; the Trinity is the best synthesis of *all* the biblical strands;
  and, properly stated, it is not a formal contradiction.

**Weigh.**
- *Textual fit, Qur'an → tawhid:* **very strong** — explicit and internally
  consistent.
- *Textual fit, Bible → Trinity:* **partial** — the Trinity is the
  *synthesizing* doctrine of a genuinely **two-stranded** text; it is
  inferred and developed, not stated; the strongest single proof-text is
  not original.
- *Textual fit, Bible → strict unitarianism:* **also partial** — it must
  explain away the high-Christology strand, which is early and real.
- *Logical coherence:* strict monotheism is **simpler and not
  contradictory**; the Trinity is **not formally contradictory but is
  metaphysically heavy and internally contested**.

**Judge.**
- On **"which view does each scripture's own text most directly
  support?"**: the **Qur'an unambiguously supports strict monotheism**
  (clear win on its own text). The **Bible does not unambiguously support
  either** in stated form — it supports a *cumulative* Trinitarian reading
  better than a strict-unitarian one *only because* of the early
  high-Christology strand, while *not* stating the creedal doctrine
  itself. So: Qur'an → strict monotheism, **decisively on the text**;
  Bible → Trinity, **by synthesis, not by explicit statement**.
- On **"is the Trinity logically incoherent?"**: the strong charge
  (it is a flat contradiction) **fails**; the weaker charge (it is not
  self-evident and rests on contested metaphysics absent from the texts)
  **succeeds**.
- The honest arbiter result: this section is **not symmetric with the
  transmission sections**. Here the Qur'an's position is *textually
  cleaner*, while the Bible's position is *textually richer but
  doctrinally constructed*. Which one counts as a *virtue* depends on a
  prior, non-historical assumption about whether revelation should be
  simple or developed — and **that** the arbiter cannot decide
  (Methodology §5).
