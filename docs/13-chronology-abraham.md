# Chronology 4 — Abraham

## The Bible's text — what is actually said

- Gen 12–25: the call, the covenant, circumcision. **Ishmael** (by Hagar)
  is blessed and made a great nation, **but the covenant runs through
  Isaac** (Gen 17:19–21).
- **The binding (Gen 22):** "Take your son, **your only son Isaac, whom
  you love**…"; the three-day journey; "**God will provide** the lamb";
  the knife stayed; the **ram** caught in the thicket; the oath-blessing
  "**in your offspring all nations shall be blessed**" (22:16–18). The son
  is **named, four times, explicitly — Isaac.**
- **NT use:** Abraham's faith "**reckoned as righteousness**" *before* the
  law and circumcision (Rom 4; Gen 15:6) — the keystone of the
  justification-by-faith argument; the offering as foreshadowing
  (Heb 11:17–19); faith proven by works (James 2:21–23); the Hagar/Sarah
  allegory (Gal 4).

## The Qur'an's text — what is actually said

- **Natural-theology scene:** the young Ibrahim reasons from a star, the
  moon, the sun ("I love not those that set") to the Creator (Q 6:74–79)
  — a rare explicit *argument* for monotheism, not mere assertion.
- He **shatters the idols** and survives the fire (Q 21:51–70; 37:97).
- **The offering (Q 37:100–113):** the **dream** — "O my son, I see in a
  dream that I sacrifice you; **what do you think?**"; the son's
  submission — "**do as you are commanded; you will find me patient**";
  the ransom of "**a great sacrifice**" (*dhibḥin ʿaẓīm*) — the basis of
  **ʿId al-Aḍḥa**. The son is **unnamed** at the offering ("a forbearing
  boy", *ghulām ḥalīm*).
- Ibrahim and **Ismaʿil raise the foundations of the Kaʿba** (Q 2:127);
  Ibrahim settles offspring in a "barren valley" (Q 14:37 — read as
  Mecca) and prays "**raise among them a messenger**" (Q 2:129 — read as
  Muhammad). Ibrahim is a *ḥanīf*, "**neither Jew nor Christian**" but a
  *muslim* (Q 3:67).

## Collision

1. **Who was offered?** The Bible is **explicit (Isaac)**. The Qurʾan is
   **silent at the offering**; the "Ishmael" answer is supplied by tafsīr.
2. **Covenant line:** Bible → Isaac/Israel; Qurʾan → Ibrahim as the
   proto-*muslim*, the **Kaʿba/Hajj/Mecca** origin, and the prayed-for
   messenger (no biblical parallel).
3. "**Abraham was a muslim**" (Q 3:67) vs. Abraham the father of Israel
   and of justifying faith (Gen; Rom 4).

## The strongest Muslim textual argument — stated fairly

Honesty (and symmetry) require giving the **best** Muslim case, not a
strawman:

- In Q 37 the **good news of Isaac (37:112) comes *after* the sacrifice
  narrative (37:101–107)**. The internal argument: the son already born
  and offered must therefore be the **earlier** son — **Ishmael** — with
  Isaac announced as a *subsequent* reward. This is a **non-trivial
  ordering argument from the text itself**, not mere tradition.
- The counter-weights: the Qurʾan still **never names** the offered son;
  several **major early Muslim authorities held it was Isaac**; and the
  Bible is **fourfold explicit (Isaac)** including the loaded "**your only
  son**" (true of Isaac as the covenant son; true of Ishmael only before
  Isaac's birth).

**Net:** the Qurʾan is **genuinely ambiguous** with a **real but
inconclusive internal argument**; the Bible is **explicit**. This is more
balanced than "pure tradition vs. text" — but the explicitness still lies
with the Bible.

## Arbiter verdict — Abraham

**Present.** Shared patriarch and monotheistic exemplar. Bible: explicit
Isaac, covenant through Isaac. Qur'an: unnamed son, Ibrahim as universal
*hanif*, Kaaba foundation.

**Weigh.**
- *Textual explicitness:* the **Bible names the son four times (Isaac)**;
  the **Qurʾan never names him at the offering**. The Muslim case is **not
  pure tradition** — the Q 37 ordering argument is real — but it is
  **inconclusive**, and early Muslim authorities were themselves split.
- *Unattested salvation-history claims, both sides:* the Kaʿba's Abrahamic
  construction, Mecca, and "Abraham was a muslim" have **no pre-Islamic or
  biblical corroboration**; the covenant-through-Isaac/Israel reading is
  likewise a **theological claim**. Both are **faith claims**, not history
  (Methodology §1).
- *Not a corruption proof either way:* differing emphases here do not, by
  themselves, prove tampering of either text.

**Judge.** On **"which text actually states who was offered?"**: the
**Bible** wins on **explicitness** (Isaac, fourfold, with "your only
son"); the Qurʾan is **deliberately ambiguous** and the Ishmael reading,
while supported by a **legitimate internal ordering argument**, is **not
decisively the Qurʾan's own statement**. On the **theological
repurposing** (proto-*muslim*, Kaʿba, the prayed-for messenger): these are
**unfalsifiable faith claims** with no external support — recorded as
**claims, not data** (out of remit). Net: a measured point to **textual
precision** on the Bible's side, with the Muslim ordering argument
**fairly credited** as more than tradition — and a flag that *both*
scriptures here build large, unverifiable salvation-history on the same
patriarch.
