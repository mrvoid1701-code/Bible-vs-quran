# Chronology 4 — Abraham

## The Bible's text

- Gen 12–25: the call, the covenant, circumcision. **Ishmael** (by Hagar)
  is blessed and made a great nation, **but the covenant runs through
  Isaac** (by Sarah): "My covenant I will establish with Isaac"
  (Gen 17:19–21).
- **The binding (Gen 22):** the son to be offered is **named explicitly —
  Isaac.**

## The Qur'an's text

- Ibrahim shatters the idols and argues against his people (Q 21:51–70;
  6:74–79).
- **The sacrificed son is *unnamed*** in the sacrifice passage: a
  "forbearing boy" (*ghulam halim*), the dream, the submission, the
  ransom (Q 37:99–113). The text does **not** state Isaac *or* Ishmael at
  the moment of the offering.
- **Classical Muslim consensus → Ishmael**, but early exegetes were
  **divided** (several major early authorities said *Isaac*); the
  Ishmael identification is largely **post-Qur'anic tafsir**, not the
  Qur'anic wording itself.
- Ibrahim and **Ismail raise the foundations of the Kaaba** (Q 2:127);
  Ibrahim is a *hanif*, "**neither Jew nor Christian**" but a *muslim*
  (submitter) (Q 3:67).

## Collision

1. **Who was offered?** The Bible is **explicit (Isaac)**. The Qur'an is
   **silent**; the Ishmael answer is **tradition filling a textual gap**.
   By the dossier's own rule (Methodology §1: text > tradition), this is a
   case where one side's confident claim is **exegetical, not scriptural**
   — and that must be stated honestly **against the Muslim popular
   claim**, just as the Bible's spurious Trinity proof-text was stated
   against the Christian one.
2. **Covenant line:** Bible → Isaac/Israel; Qur'an → Ibrahim as the
   proto-*muslim* and the **Kaaba/Hajj** origin (no biblical parallel).
3. "**Abraham was a muslim**" (Q 3:67) vs. Abraham as father of Israel and
   of faith (Gen; Rom 4).

## Arbiter verdict — Abraham

**Present.** Shared patriarch and monotheistic exemplar. Bible: explicit
Isaac, covenant through Isaac. Qur'an: unnamed son, Ibrahim as universal
*hanif*, Kaaba foundation.

**Weigh.**
- *Textual explicitness:* the **Bible names the son**; the **Qur'an does
  not**. The famous "it was Ishmael" is **tradition**, and saying so is
  required by symmetry — the same scalpel used on the *Comma Johanneum*.
- *Unattested historical claims:* the Kaaba's Abrahamic construction and
  "Abraham was a muslim" have **no pre-Islamic documentary or biblical
  corroboration**; they are **faith claims**, not history (Methodology
  §1).
- *Not a corruption proof either way:* differing emphases here do not, by
  themselves, prove tampering of either text.

**Judge.** On **"which text actually says who was offered?"**: the
**Bible** wins on explicitness; the Muslim Ishmael claim is, strictly,
**extra-Qur'anic**. On the **theological repurposing** (proto-*muslim*,
Kaaba): these are **unfalsifiable faith claims** with no external
support — the arbiter records them as **claims, not data**, and rules
neither true nor false (out of remit). Net: a point to **textual
precision** on the Bible's side, and a flag that popular Muslim apologetics
here repeats exactly the *tradition-as-text* error the dossier charged
against Christian apologetics.
