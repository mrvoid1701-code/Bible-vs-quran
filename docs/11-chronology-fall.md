# Chronology 2 — The Fall and the Origin of Sin

This is the **single most consequential divergence in the whole study**:
the same garden scene yields **opposite anthropologies**, and from that
fork everything downstream (atonement, the cross, salvation at the end)
follows by logical necessity.

## The Bible's text

- Gen 3: the serpent, the eating, the expulsion.
- The NT builds the doctrine of **inherited sin**: "through one man sin
  entered the world… death spread to all" (Rom 5:12–19); "in sin did my
  mother conceive me" (Ps 51:5). Humanity inherits a **corrupted nature**
  and stands in need of **redemption** it cannot self-supply.
- Eve is "deceived" first (1 Tim 2:14); Adam's act is the federal one.

## The Qur'an's text

- Adam and his spouse are both tempted by **Shaytan/Iblis**, both eat
  (Q 7:19–25; 20:120–122). The blame is **shared**; there is **no "Eve
  caused it"** framing.
- Crucially: "Then Adam **received words from his Lord**, and He **accepted
  his repentance**" (Q 2:37). The lapse is **forgiven on the spot**.
- **No original sin, no inherited guilt:** "**no bearer of burdens bears
  the burden of another**" (Q 35:18; 6:164; 53:38). Each soul is born in
  *fitra* (an upright primordial nature) and is accountable only for its
  own deeds.
- The descent to earth is presented within the plan announced beforehand —
  Adam as *khalifa* (Q 2:30).

## Collision

| Question | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Is guilt inherited? | Yes (original sin) | **No** (Q 35:18) |
| Is human nature corrupted by the Fall? | Yes | No (*fitra* intact) |
| Was the first sin immediately forgiven? | No — sets up redemption | **Yes** (Q 2:37) |
| Is a redeemer therefore required? | Yes | No |
| Is Eve singled out? | Comparatively yes | No — shared |

This is **not** an emphasis difference. It is a binary fork. **Original
sin is the reason Christianity needs a cross**; its **absence** is why
Islam needs none — exactly the forgiveness-mechanism split already
isolated in [cap. 09 §2.4](09-nature-of-god.md).

## Logic

- The Islamic principle "**no one bears another's burden**" is **both a
  text and a clean ethical axiom** — it matches the common moral intuition
  that guilt is not transferable.
- The Christian reply: original sin is usually framed (in the more
  defensible models) as **inherited corruption/mortality**, not imputed
  personal *guilt* — answering the ethical objection — and it has
  **explanatory power** for the empirically observable universality of
  human wrongdoing.
- Both are internally coherent; they answer **different questions** (Islam:
  *is transferred guilt just?* — no; Christianity: *why is everyone, in
  fact, a sinner?* — inherited corruption).

## Arbiter verdict — the Fall

**Present.** Same narrative skeleton (couple, tempter, transgression,
consequence); opposite doctrines of what it did to humanity.

**Weigh.**
- *Strong (Islam, textual + ethical):* Q 35:18's non-transfer of guilt is
  explicit and ethically intuitive; immediate forgiveness (Q 2:37) is
  explicit.
- *Strong (Christianity, explanatory):* original sin (as corrupted nature)
  accounts for universal moral failure and grounds the entire redemptive
  arc.
- *Decisive structural fact:* this fork **necessitates** every later
  divergence about atonement and salvation. It is the **headwater of the
  seam**.

**Judge.** On **ethical parsimony of the guilt question**: advantage
**Qur'an** — non-transferable guilt is both its plain text and the
stronger stand-alone moral principle. On **explanatory scope for the human
condition**: advantage **Bible** — it offers a reason *why* all sin, which
the Qur'an does not attempt. Which job a true revelation "should" do —
deliver a clean ethic or explain the empirical fact of sin — is the
non-arbitrable value premise. What is **not** in doubt: from here, the two
systems are **logically committed** to diverge all the way to the end.
