# Chronology 2 — The Fall and the Origin of Sin

This is the **single most consequential divergence in the whole study**:
the same garden scene yields **opposite anthropologies**, and from that
fork everything downstream (atonement, the cross, salvation at the end)
follows by logical necessity.

## The Bible's text — what is actually said

- **The act:** the serpent's "you will be like God" (Gen 3:5); they eat.
- **God's words:** "**Where are you?**" (3:9) — the summons; then the
  interrogation, the man blaming the woman, the woman blaming the serpent.
- **The curses** (3:14–19): the serpent, the pain of childbirth, the
  cursed ground, "**dust you are, and to dust you shall return**."
- **Gen 3:15 — the *protoevangelium*:** "he shall bruise your head, and
  you shall bruise his heel" — read by Christians, **retrospectively**, as
  the first thread of redemption. *(By the dossier's symmetry rule this
  retrospective reading is flagged exactly like "Let Us make man" in
  [cap. 10](10-chronology-creation.md): suggestive within the cumulative
  case, not a stand-alone proof.)*
- **Expulsion:** driven out, the cherubim and flaming sword guard the
  **tree of life** (3:24) — access to life is now barred.
- **The doctrine that grows from it:** "through one man sin entered… death
  spread to all" (Rom 5:12–19); "in sin did my mother conceive me"
  (Ps 51:5). This was sharpened by **Augustine vs. Pelagius** (5th c.)
  into Western **original sin**. Note the **internal Christian split**:
  Eastern Orthodoxy teaches **ancestral sin** — inherited *mortality and
  corruption*, **not** inherited personal *guilt* — which is materially
  closer to the Islamic objection than the Augustinian/Reformed form.
- Eve is "deceived" first (1 Tim 2:14); **Adam's** act is the *federal*
  one (Rom 5 makes Adam, not Eve, the head).

## The Qur'an's text — what is actually said

- Both Adam and his spouse are tempted by **Iblis/Shaytan**, both eat
  (Q 7:19–25; 20:120–122); blame is **shared**, with **no "Eve caused
  it"** framing.
- **Their confession is the model penitent prayer:** "**Our Lord, we have
  wronged ourselves; if You do not forgive us… we will be among the
  losers**" (Q 7:23) — they own it, blaming no one.
- **Satan's vow** sets up earthly life as a moral test: "I will surely
  lead them astray…" (Q 7:16–17; 15:39; 38:82); "**Descend**, as enemies
  to one another" (Q 2:36).
- **Forgiven on the spot:** "Then Adam **received words from his Lord**,
  and He **accepted his repentance**" (Q 2:37); "then his Lord **chose
  him**, turned to him, and **guided**" (Q 20:122).
- **No original sin, no inherited guilt:** "**no bearer of burdens bears
  the burden of another**" (Q 35:18; 6:164; 53:38). Each soul is born in
  *fiṭra* and answers only for its own deeds. *(Tradition even makes Adam
  a prophet, raising the ['isma](15-chronology-david-prophets.md)
  question — flagged as tradition, not Qurʾanic text.)*
- The descent is **within the announced plan** — Adam as *khalifa*
  (Q 2:30).

## Collision

| Question | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Is guilt inherited? | Yes (original sin) | **No** (Q 35:18) |
| Is human nature corrupted by the Fall? | Yes | No (*fitra* intact) |
| Was the first sin immediately forgiven? | No — sets up redemption | **Yes** (Q 2:37) |
| Is a redeemer therefore required? | Yes | No |
| Is Eve singled out? | Comparatively yes | No — shared |

This is **not** an emphasis difference. It is a binary fork. **Original
sin is the reason Christianity needs a cross**; its **absence** is why
Islam needs none — exactly the forgiveness-mechanism split already
isolated in [cap. 09 §2.4](09-nature-of-god.md).

## The Bible against itself — Ezekiel 18

Symmetry demands a point that *helps the Muslim case* and must therefore
be stated plainly: **the Bible itself contains Q 35:18's principle.**

> "The soul that sins shall die. **The son shall not bear the iniquity of
> the father**, nor the father the iniquity of the son" (Ezek 18:20; cf.
> Deut 24:16; Jer 31:30).

So "no one bears another's burden" is **not unique to the Qur'an** — it is
*also biblical*, and it sits in **internal tension** with Rom 5's inherited
death. Christian theology reconciles this by distinguishing **inherited
corruption/mortality** (a *condition*, transmitted) from **personal guilt**
(a *verdict*, not transferred) — and via **federal headship** (Adam and
Christ as covenant representatives, Rom 5:12–21). Whether that
reconciliation is convincing is contestable; that the tension is *internal
to the Bible* is simply true, and pretending otherwise would breach the
symmetry rule (Methodology §3).

## The textual bridge — Adam to Christ

The load-bearing verses that make the cross *necessary* in the Christian
system are explicit and must be named:

> "As in **Adam** all die, so also in **Christ** shall all be made alive"
> (1 Cor 15:22); "as by one man's disobedience the many were made sinners,
> so by one man's obedience the many will be made righteous" (Rom 5:19).

This Adam–Christ typology is the **hinge of the entire seam**: the Fall
creates the deficit; the cross ([cap. 16](16-chronology-jesus.md)) pays
it; the verdict at the end ([cap. 18](18-chronology-eschatology.md))
applies it. Remove inherited death and the bridge has nothing to span —
which is precisely the Islamic position.

## Logic

- "**No one bears another's burden**" is a **clean ethical axiom** and —
  per Ezek 18 — is **affirmed by both scriptures**. The Muslim cannot
  claim it as a Qurʾan-only insight; the Christian cannot deny it is
  biblical.
- The genuine divergence is **narrower than usually stated**: not "is
  transferred *guilt* just?" (both texts say no), but "**is human nature
  itself inherited-corrupt, requiring a redeemer?**" Bible: yes
  (Rom 5, federal headship). Qurʾan: no (*fiṭra* intact, Q 2:37 closes the
  matter).
- The Christian model has **explanatory power** for the empirically
  universal fact of wrongdoing; the Qurʾanic model has **ethical
  cleanliness** and needs no atonement apparatus. Each answers a
  *different* question and is internally coherent.

## Arbiter verdict — the Fall

**Present.** Same narrative skeleton (couple, tempter, transgression,
consequence); opposite doctrines of what it did to humanity.

**Weigh.**
- *Symmetric correction:* the "no one bears another's burden" principle is
  **biblical too** (Ezek 18:20) — so it is **not** a clean Qurʾan-vs-Bible
  win; it is, in part, a **Bible-internal tension**.
- *Strong (Islam, textual + ethical):* immediate forgiveness (Q 2:37) and
  *fiṭra* are explicit; no atonement apparatus is needed.
- *Strong (Christianity, explanatory + textual):* the Adam–Christ bridge
  (Rom 5; 1 Cor 15) is explicit and gives the system its reason for the
  cross; original sin (esp. the Orthodox *corruption-not-guilt* form)
  answers the universality of wrongdoing.
- *Decisive structural fact:* this fork **necessitates** every later
  divergence about atonement and salvation — the **headwater of the
  seam**.

**Judge.** The popular framing ("the Bible unjustly transfers guilt; the
Qurʾan does not") is **too crude and partly false**: *both* texts reject
transferred personal guilt (Ezek 18 = Q 35:18). The **real, narrower**
divergence is **inherited corruption requiring a redeemer (Bible)** vs.
**an intact nature needing none (Qurʾan)**. On **ethical parsimony**:
advantage **Qurʾan** — fewer moving parts, and its principle is even
conceded by the Bible. On **explanatory scope and textual integration**:
advantage **Bible** — the Adam–Christ typology is explicit and load-
bearing. Which a true revelation *should* prioritise — a clean ethic or an
explanation of universal sin — is the **non-arbitrable value premise**
(Methodology §5). Not in doubt: from here the two systems are **logically
committed** to diverge all the way to the end.
