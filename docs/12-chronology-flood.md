# Chronology 3 — The Flood and Noah

## The Bible's text — what is actually said

- **God's grief:** "the LORD was sorry that He had made man… and was
  grieved in His heart" (Gen 6:6) — strongly anthropopathic language (cf.
  the [cap. 09](09-nature-of-god.md) / [cap. 10](10-chronology-creation.md)
  contrast with Q 50:38's transcendence).
- **Noah "found grace"** (6:8); precise ark instructions; **40 days** of
  rain, waters prevailing **150 days**; **all flesh dies** except **eight
  souls** — Noah, his wife, his three sons (Shem, Ham, Japheth) and their
  wives. **The whole household is saved.**
- The raven and the **dove**; the altar; God "smelled the pleasing aroma"
  and resolved "**never again**" (8:21).
- **The Noahic covenant** (Gen 9): universal, with **all humanity and
  every creature**, sealed by the **bow in the cloud** — a covenant of
  *preservation/common grace*. *(Gen 9:20–27, the drunkenness and the
  curse of Canaan, is a separate, much-abused text; noted neutrally, not
  germane to the flood comparison.)*

## The Qur'an's text — what is actually said

- Nuh preaches **"950 years"** (Q 29:14) and is **mocked while building**
  the ark (Q 11:38); builds it under inspiration (Q 11:37).
- Onset signal: "**the oven gushed forth**" (*fāra al-tannūr*, Q 11:40);
  the ark "**ran amid waves like mountains**" (Q 11:42); at the end the
  command "**O earth, swallow your water; O sky, withhold**" (Q 11:44).
- **The son-dialogue** is given in full: the son says "I will take refuge
  on a mountain"; he **drowns** (Q 11:42–43). Noah pleads "**my son is of
  my family**"; God corrects him — "**he is not of your family; it is an
  unrighteous deed; ask Me not of that you know nothing of**" (Q 11:45–46);
  **Noah repents** (11:47). *(Even a prophet is here corrected — touching
  the ['isma](15-chronology-david-prophets.md) question; flagged as a
  textual datum, not resolved.)*
- **Noah's wife** is a Qurʾanic **parable of the unbelieving spouse**,
  paired with Lot's wife (Q 66:10) — **lost** though married to a prophet.
- Scope (global vs. local) is **left open** and debated in tafsir.

## Collision

The decisive divergence is **who was saved**:

| | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Noah's wife | saved | **lost** (Q 66:10) |
| Noah's sons | all three saved | **one son drowns** (Q 11:43) |
| Salvation principle | household / lineage | **faith over kinship** |

The Qur'an's version is internally purposeful: it **dramatizes the
[cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md) principle** — no one is saved by family
tie; each stands on personal faith ("he is not of your family; his conduct
is unrighteous", Q 11:46). It reads as a **theological correction** of
Genesis's household-salvation motif, fully consistent with Q 35:18 and
Ezek 18:20 — note the Qurʾan is here **more consistent with the shared
individual-responsibility principle** than Genesis's household model is.

## The shared external problem — Mesopotamian flood traditions

Symmetry requires naming a difficulty that lands on **both** texts:
mainstream scholarship sees the Genesis flood as **literarily related** to
older Mesopotamian accounts (the **Gilgamesh** XI tablet, *Atrahasis*,
Ziusudra) — shared motifs: the warned righteous man, the ark, the birds
sent out, the mountain landing, the sacrifice. The Qurʾanic account, being
later, inherits the same tradition-stream.

This is presented **neutrally**, not as a weapon: it is a **common
external pressure** (Methodology §3, §5). It does **not** favour either
scripture against the other, and the arbiter does **not** rule on literary
dependence vs. independent memory of a real event — out of remit.

## Two theological functions

The divergence is not only "who survived" but **what the story is for**:

- **Bible → covenant of preservation.** The flood culminates in the
  **Noahic covenant** with all humanity and creation (the rainbow): a
  theology of **common grace and divine restraint** ("never again").
- **Qurʾan → serialized warning.** Nuh is the first in a **repeating
  "warner" pattern** (Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ, Shuʿayb): a messenger is mocked,
  the people are destroyed, the faithful saved. The flood is one episode
  in a **didactic series on rejecting prophets**, not the seal of a
  universal covenant.

Both are internally coherent; they are **different literary-theological
machines** built from the same raw narrative.

## Arbiter verdict — the Flood

**Present.** Shared event (righteous Noah, ark, deluge, deliverance);
divergent on the survivors and on the *principle* the story teaches.

**Weigh.**
- *Symmetric, out of remit:* a **global flood** is rejected by mainstream
  geology/archaeology, **and** the Mesopotamian parallels pressure the
  Genesis account's originality — **both** pressures fall on the two texts
  together (Methodology §3, §5) and decide nothing between them.
- *Real divergence, internally consistent:* the lost son and wife are
  **deliberate** in the Qurʾan, locking the flood onto the same
  individual-responsibility seam as the Fall — and arguably **more
  consistent** with the shared Ezek 18 / Q 35:18 principle than Genesis's
  household salvation.
- *Different machines:* covenant-of-preservation (Bible) vs. serialized
  warner-pattern (Qurʾan) — a structural, not cosmetic, difference.

**Judge.** On **historicity and originality**: a **wash** — both face the
same external problems; no winner. On **internal-principle consistency**:
a *narrow* point to the **Qurʾan** — its faith-over-kinship outcome aligns
better with the individual-responsibility axiom both scriptures elsewhere
affirm, whereas Genesis's whole-household salvation sits in mild tension
with its own Ezek 18. On **theological function**: not better/worse but
**different by design** (covenant vs. warning-series), each coherent. No
evidentiary winner overall; a clear, traceable continuation of the seam.
