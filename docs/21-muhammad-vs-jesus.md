# Focused Question — The Teachings of Muhammad vs. Jesus (and the Two Systems)

By request: Muhammad and his doctrines, Christianity as a system, and a
like-for-like comparison of what Muhammad and Jesus actually taught. The
dossier's rules apply — sources weighed by date, claims tested
symmetrically, value-premises left to the reader.

## 1. The sources problem (stated first, for both)

- **Jesus' teaching:** the only primary witnesses are the **first-century
  Gospels** — early, but **theologically shaped** (esp. John's
  high-Christology sayings; see [cap. 05](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md)).
- **Muhammad's teaching:** three layers — the **Qurʾan** (earliest,
  ~7th c.), then **Ḥadīth** (compiled 2nd–3rd c. AH, with an entire
  Muslim science of authentication, *and* much disputed material), then
  the **Sīra** (Ibn Isḥāq via Ibn Hishām, al-Ṭabarī — written ~150+ years
  later). Mainstream historians (Sean Anthony, Fred Donner) treat the
  Qurʾanic layer as primary and the Sīra cautiously.

Neither founder's words reach us unmediated. **Symmetry:** a skeptic's
standard for the Gospels must also apply to Ḥadīth/Sīra, and vice versa.

## 2. Muhammad's core doctrines

- **Tawḥīd:** absolute oneness of God; *shirk* the gravest sin.
- **Prophethood & finality:** a line of prophets (Adam→Jesus), Muhammad
  the **seal** (Q 33:40); the Qurʾan as God's literal, final speech.
- **Worship & law (the *dīn*):** the **Five Pillars** (creed, prayer,
  alms, fasting, pilgrimage) and a comprehensive **Sharīʿa** covering
  worship, family, contracts, criminal and political life.
- **Community & polity:** the *umma*; Muhammad was simultaneously
  **prophet, statesman, judge, and military commander** (Medina period).
- **Ethics:** charity (*zakāt/ṣadaqa*), care for orphans and the poor,
  honesty, justice, sexual morality, accountability at the Judgment,
  mercy (*raḥma*) as God's foremost trait.
- **Regulated warfare (*qitāl/jihād*):** a contested topic — the Qurʾan
  has both "**no compulsion in religion**" (Q 2:256) and the "sword"
  verses (Q 9:5; 9:29) in the context of 7th-c. Arabian conflict; Muslim
  scholarship splits between **contextual/defensive** and
  **universalist** readings, and over **abrogation**. Presented as a
  genuine internal debate, not a settled polemic.

## 3. Jesus' teaching (per the Gospels)

- **The Kingdom of God** is the center: it has drawn near; repent and
  believe.
- **The Sermon on the Mount:** internalization of Torah ("you have heard…
  but I say"), the Beatitudes, **love of enemies**, **non-retaliation**
  ("turn the other cheek"), radical forgiveness, secret almsgiving.
- **Two great commands:** love God (the *Shema*, Mark 12:29–30) and love
  neighbor; the Golden Rule.
- **Grace and the lost:** parables of the prodigal, the lost sheep;
  forgiveness of sinners; warnings to the self-righteous.
- **His own role:** "Son of Man" sayings, authority to forgive sins, the
  cross self-interpreted as "a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45) — the
  high-self-claims being **contested within scholarship**.
- **Religion and power:** **no law code, no army, no state** — "**my
  kingdom is not of this world**" (John 18:36); executed by Rome, not
  ruling it.

## 4. Like-for-like comparison

| Dimension | Jesus (Gospels) | Muhammad (Qurʾan/Sīra) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary role | itinerant teacher-healer; suffering Messiah | prophet **and** statesman, judge, commander |
| Core message | Kingdom of God, grace, atonement | *tawḥīd*, final revelation, a God-ordered society |
| Ethic of force | **non-resistance**, enemy-love | regulated, sometimes offensive, warfare |
| Law | reinterprets Torah; no new code | a comprehensive **Sharīʿa** |
| Religion & state | separated ("not of this world") | **integrated** (*dīn wa dawla* in practice) |
| Salvation | by grace through his atoning work | deeds under God's mercy, no atonement |
| Shared ethics | monotheism, charity, mercy, justice, care for the poor, sexual morality, judgment to come | same list — substantial overlap |

The **ethical overlap is large**; the **structural divergence** is the
*type of founder*: a non-resistant suffering teacher vs. a prophet-
statesman who also legislated and led in war.

## 5. The symmetry traps (both polemics fail here)

- **"Muhammad waged war, Jesus did not."** True of the *two individuals*.
  But the honest comparison of **scriptural systems** must set Muhammad
  beside the **Hebrew Bible's prophets and lawgivers** — Moses, Joshua,
  David — and the Torah's **divinely-commanded *ḥērem*** (Deut 20:16–17;
  1 Sam 15:3, "kill man and woman, infant and nursling"). By that
  like-for-like measure the Bible's sanctioned warfare is **at least as
  severe**. Christian polemic that cites the Qurʾan's war verses while
  ignoring Deut 20 / 1 Sam 15 **fails the symmetry rule** (Methodology
  §3).
- **"The Bible is violent, so Christianity is false."** The Muslim mirror-
  polemic fails identically: OT war texts read out of their context, and
  Jesus' non-resistance ignored.
- **"As practiced" cuts both ways and is not the text.** Crusades/
  Inquisition vs. conquests — *communities*, not *scriptures*. The dossier
  judges **texts**, and flags "as-practiced" arguments as off-target on
  **both** sides.

The defensible residue: **Jesus' personal recorded ethic is distinctively
non-resistant; Muhammad's recorded career is that of an armed prophet-
statesman — but so is Moses'/David's.** So the sharp contrast is
**Jesus-the-individual vs. Muhammad-the-individual**; at the **system
level** (Torah+prophets vs. Qurʾan+Sunna) the gap **narrows sharply**.

## 6. Christianity and Islam as *systems* (not just two men)

- **Christianity** is constitutively about the **person and work of
  Christ** — incarnation, atonement, resurrection, Trinity, grace, church
  and sacraments. Its "law" is reinterpreted Torah plus the law of love;
  it has **no divinely-mandated polity**.
- **Islam** is a ***dīn*** — creed *plus* law *plus* community: right
  belief (*ʿaqīda*), a revealed legal system (*Sharīʿa*), and a social
  order. Salvation is by God's mercy upon faith and deeds.
- So they are **different *kinds* of thing**: Christianity centers on a
  **person to be united with**; Islam on a **revealed pattern of life to
  be followed**. Most "who is better" arguments quietly assume the rival's
  category — a **category error** (Methodology §4).

## Arbiter verdict — Muhammad vs. Jesus, and the systems

**Present.** Polemics: "Jesus taught love, Muhammad taught the sword" vs.
"the Bible is more violent than the Qurʾan." Each is a one-sided cut.

**Weigh.**
- *Large shared ethic:* monotheism, charity, mercy, justice, sexual
  morality, final judgment — substantively overlapping.
- *Real structural divergence:* **type of founder** — non-resistant
  suffering teacher vs. prophet-statesman-lawgiver. Textually clear,
  internally coherent on each side.
- *Symmetry decisive on "violence":* Muhammad vs. **Moses/Joshua/David +
  Torah ḥērem** is the like-for-like test; the bare "Muhammad was violent"
  polemic **fails** because the Bible's sanctioned warfare is at least as
  severe. *Jesus-the-individual's* non-resistance is a **genuine** and
  **distinctive** datum — but it is *Jesus vs. Muhammad*, not
  *Christianity vs. Islam* as institutions.
- *Source symmetry:* Gospels (early, shaped) and Ḥadīth/Sīra (later,
  authentication-debated) **both** need critical handling.

**Judge.**
- On the **shared ethical core**: a **draw** — broadly common.
- On **"whose teaching is more peaceful?"**: at the **individual** level,
  a **nameable** point — **Jesus' recorded personal ethic is
  distinctively non-resistant**; at the **system/scripture** level
  (Torah+prophets vs. Qurʾan+Sunna) the contrast **collapses toward
  parity** by the symmetry rule. The popular knockout therefore **only
  works if the comparison is rigged** (Jesus vs. Muhammad, never Moses vs.
  Muhammad).
- On **"which kind of founder/system is *better*?"** — suffering
  non-resistant redeemer vs. prophet-legislator-statesman — this is a
  **value premise** (what a true revelation *should* be), and the arbiter
  **declines** it (Methodology §5).
- The salvation/identity divergence underneath is the **same single seam**
  ([cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md)/[16](16-chronology-jesus.md)) — not a
  new one.
