# Chronology 6 — David, Solomon, and the Portrayal of God's Chosen

This chapter isolates a **systematic pattern**, not a single episode: the
two texts portray God's chosen servants on **opposite philosophies**.

## The Bible's text

- **David:** adultery with Bathsheba and the engineered death of Uriah
  (2 Sam 11), exposed and rebuked (2 Sam 12), penitential Ps 51.
- **Solomon:** in old age his foreign wives turn his heart; he builds high
  places for other gods (1 Kings 11).
- The pattern is general: patriarchs, prophets, and kings are shown
  **"warts and all"** — deceit, violence, idolatry, doubt.

## The Qur'an's text

- **Dawud** and **Sulayman** are **righteous prophets**. The Bathsheba/
  Uriah episode is **absent**; Q 38:21–25 (the two disputants) has Dawud
  seek forgiveness, but **no adultery or murder is narrated**.
- **Sulayman** is "an excellent servant" (Q 38:30); his alleged idolatry
  is **not affirmed** (Q 2:102 transfers blame to devils, not Solomon).
- General doctrine: **prophetic impeccability (*'isma*)** — prophets are
  divinely protected from grave sin.

## Collision

This is the clearest **"different by design"** divergence in the dossier:

| | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Portrayal of the chosen | flawed, sinning humans | morally elevated, protected |
| David's adultery/murder | narrated and condemned | not narrated |
| Solomon's idolatry | narrated | denied/deflected |
| Governing doctrine | grace despite sin | *'isma* (impeccability) |

## Logic — and why it matters methodologically

- The Bible's embarrassing detail engages the **criterion of
  embarrassment**: in historiography (and in mainstream historical-Jesus
  scholarship), a tradition that **damages its own heroes** is *less
  likely to be invented* and is weighed as a mark of **authenticity**.
  This is a recognized neutral-historian tool, not a Christian apologetic
  invention.
- *'isma* is a **theological axiom** about what *befits* a prophet — it is
  **not a historical claim** and is **not falsifiable** as such.
- Both are internally coherent. But they are **different *kinds* of
  claim**: one invites historical assessment; the other states a doctrine.

## Arbiter verdict — the chosen

**Present.** Bible: fallible servants saved by grace. Qur'an: protected
servants befitting revelation.

**Weigh.**
- *Methodological weight, Bible:* the **criterion of embarrassment** is a
  standard, neutral historiographic tool and **favours the non-invention**
  of the unflattering accounts.
- *Theological axiom, Qur'an:* *'isma* is coherent **as doctrine** but
  contributes **no historical evidence**; it explains the divergence
  rather than evidencing it.
- *Symmetry check:* this is **not** a corruption proof against either —
  it is a difference of **portrayal philosophy**.

**Judge.** On **historical-evidentiary value of the narratives**:
advantage **Bible** — by the very neutral criterion historians apply, its
self-damaging detail is harder to dismiss as invention. On **internal
theological fittingness**: the Qur'an's elevated prophetology is
**coherent on its own terms**. Whether revelation *should* embarrass its
heroes (authenticity) or protect them (fittingness) is, once more, the
**non-arbitrable value premise**. This is the dossier's tidiest example of
"**different by design, not by corruption**."
