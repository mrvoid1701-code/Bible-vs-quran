# Chronology 6 — David, Solomon, and the Portrayal of God's Chosen

This chapter isolates a **systematic pattern**, not a single episode: the
two texts portray God's chosen servants on **opposite philosophies**.

## The Bible's text — what is actually said

- **David:** adultery with Bathsheba, then the engineered death of Uriah
  (2 Sam 11); Nathan's trap-parable and "**You are the man**" (2 Sam 12);
  the child dies; lifelong consequences; the penitential **Ps 51** ("a
  broken and contrite heart"). Yet David is still "**a man after God's own
  heart**" — the Bible **both** condemns *and* elevates him.
- **Solomon:** in old age his foreign wives turn his heart; he builds
  high places for other gods (1 Kgs 11) — narrated as real apostasy.
- The pattern is general: patriarchs, prophets, and kings shown **"warts
  and all"** — deceit, violence, idolatry, doubt.

## The Qur'an's text — what is actually said

- **Dāwūd** and **Sulaymān** are **righteous prophets**. The Bathsheba/
  Uriah episode is **absent**; Q 38:21–25 (the two litigants) has Dāwūd
  realise "**We had tried him**", **ask forgiveness**, and be forgiven —
  but **no adultery or murder is narrated** (classical exegetes were
  themselves uneasy about *what* the sin was, and largely **purged** the
  Bathsheba material that had entered early tafsīr as *isrāʾīliyyāt*).
- **Sulaymān** is "an excellent servant" (Q 38:30); his alleged idolatry
  is **not affirmed** (Q 2:102 shifts blame to the devils).
- Governing doctrine: **prophetic impeccability (*ʿiṣma*)**.

## The symmetry the popular argument hides

The neat "Bible: sinful prophets / Qurʾan: sinless prophets" contrast is
**partly false on the Qurʾan's own text** — and symmetry requires saying
so (cf. how [cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md) showed Ezek 18 sits *inside*
the Bible against original sin):

- The Qurʾan **itself** shows prophets erring and **seeking
  forgiveness**: Adam (Q 7:23); Mūsā after killing the Egyptian — "**my
  Lord, I have wronged myself, forgive me**" (Q 28:16); Yūnus — "**I have
  been of the wrongdoers**" (Q 21:87); Dāwūd asking forgiveness (Q 38:24);
  and Muhammad himself addressed "**ask forgiveness for your sin**"
  (Q 47:19; 48:2; the *ʿAbasa* rebuke, Q 80:1–10).
- So *ʿiṣma* (especially its strong form) is, in part, a **later
  harmonising doctrine**, not a flat Qurʾanic given. The real divergence
  is therefore **Bible vs. developed Islamic doctrine** more than **Bible
  vs. Qurʾanic text** — exactly the kind of precision the dossier applied
  to the Christian side.

## Collision

This is the clearest **"different by design"** divergence in the dossier:

| | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Portrayal of the chosen | flawed, sinning humans | morally elevated, protected |
| David's adultery/murder | narrated and condemned | not narrated |
| Solomon's idolatry | narrated | denied/deflected |
| Governing doctrine | grace despite sin | *'isma* (impeccability) |

## Logic — and why it matters methodologically

- The Bible's embarrassing detail engages the **criterion of
  embarrassment**: a tradition that **damages its own heroes** is *less
  likely to be invented* — a recognised neutral-historian tool, not a
  Christian apologetic invention. *Caveat:* the criterion is **suggestive,
  not absolute** — the unflattering detail also serves the Bible's own
  **grace theme**, so it is not pure raw history either.
- Strong *ʿiṣma* is a **theological axiom** about what *befits* a prophet
  — **not falsifiable** — and, as shown above, is **in tension with the
  Qurʾan's own text** (prophets repent). So the cleanest framing is
  **Bible-narrative vs. later-doctrine**, with the **Qurʾanic text sitting
  nearer the Bible** than the doctrine admits.
- Both systems are internally coherent; they are **different *kinds* of
  claim** — one invites historical assessment, the other states (and then
  harmonises toward) a doctrine.

## Arbiter verdict — the chosen

**Present.** Bible: fallible servants saved by grace. Qur'an: protected
servants befitting revelation.

**Weigh.**
- *Methodological weight, Bible:* the **criterion of embarrassment** is a
  standard, neutral historiographic tool and **favours the non-invention**
  of the unflattering accounts.
- *Theological axiom, Qur'an:* *'isma* is coherent **as doctrine** but
  contributes **no historical evidence**; it explains the divergence
  rather than evidencing it.
- *Symmetry check:* this is **not** a corruption proof against either —
  it is a difference of **portrayal philosophy**.

**Judge.** On **historical-evidentiary value**: a **measured** advantage
**Bible** — the criterion of embarrassment makes its self-damaging detail
harder to dismiss as invention, though the criterion is *suggestive, not
decisive*. On **the contrast itself**: the popular "sinful vs. sinless
prophets" framing is **partly false** — the **Qurʾanic text also has
prophets repent**; the sharp divergence is really **Bible vs. the
developed *ʿiṣma* doctrine**, with the Qurʾan's own wording **closer to
the Bible** than later theology concedes (symmetry with the Ezek 18 point
in cap. 11). Whether revelation *should* embarrass or protect its heroes
remains the **non-arbitrable value premise** (Methodology §5). Still the
dossier's tidiest case of "**different by design, not by corruption**" —
now with the added precision that the design is **doctrinal, layered over
a text that is less divergent than the slogans suggest**.
