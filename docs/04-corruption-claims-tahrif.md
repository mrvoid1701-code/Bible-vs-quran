# Corruption Claims: *Tahrif* — In Both Directions

This is the core battleground. The Islamic charge is that the Bible was
*corrupted* (*tahrif*). The symmetry principle (Methodology §3) requires
that the same charge be tested against the Qur'an.

---

## Part A — The Islamic doctrine of *tahrif*

### The Qur'anic data is double-edged

Verses cited **for** corruption:

- **Q 2:75** — some "heard the word of Allah then distorted it knowingly."
- **Q 2:79** — "woe to those who write the book with their own hands then
  say 'this is from Allah'."
- **Q 3:78** — "a party twist their tongues with the Book."
- **Q 4:46**, **Q 5:13**, **Q 5:41** — "they distort words from their
  places."

Verses that **affirm the prior scriptures** in Muhammad's own time:

- **Q 5:43** — "how do they make you a judge while they have the Torah, in
  which is the judgment of Allah?"
- **Q 5:47** — "let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah revealed
  therein."
- **Q 5:68** — "you stand on nothing until you observe the Torah and the
  Gospel."
- **Q 10:94** — "ask those who read the Scripture before you."
- **Q 3:3**, **Q 2:136** — the Torah and Gospel are confirmed.

The Qur'an itself, read internally, pushes more naturally toward
**tahrif al-maʿna** (distortion of *meaning* / concealment /
misreading by some) than toward **tahrif al-nass** (the physical text was
rewritten) — otherwise commanding people to judge by the Torah/Gospel in
the 7th c. would be incoherent.

### The classical Muslim scholars were divided

- **Tahrif al-maʿna camp** (the older, arguably majority classical view):
  many early exegetes, and notably **al-Razi**, **al-Ghazali**'s
  general tendency, and others held the corruption was interpretive /
  concealment, the text largely standing.
- **Tahrif al-nass camp:** **Ibn Hazm** (11th c.) argued aggressively for
  *textual* corruption of the Bible, cataloguing alleged contradictions —
  this became the dominant *popular* position later.
- **Ibn Khaldun** and others noted the People of the Book still possessed
  their scriptures.

### Modern popular apologetics vs. scholarship

- **Ahmed Deedat, Zakir Naik, and the popular *daʿwa* circuit** argue
  *textual* corruption, leaning heavily on **Bart Ehrman** and on the
  Comma Johanneum, Mark 16, John 7:53–8:11, etc.
- **Scholarly assessment:** the cited variants are **real but limited**
  (see [Bible Transmission](02-bible-textual-transmission.md)). They show
  imperfect copying, *not* the wholesale invention or rewriting of the
  Bible. Using Ehrman to prove "the Bible is fabricated" overreads him —
  Ehrman explicitly denies that the variants overturn the core message,
  and he is **equally a critic of traditional Qur'anic origins claims**.

## Part B — The charge run in reverse (symmetry)

If textual variation, redaction, and human transmission count as
"corruption" for the Bible, the same test applied to the Qur'an yields:

- **Destruction of rival codices** under ʿUthman: variant readings (Ibn
  Masʿud's, Ubayy's) were **physically eliminated**. By the strict
  standard, suppressing variants is not the same as never having had them.
- **The Sanaa palimpsest lower text:** a *physical* manuscript proving a
  **different early recension** existed — internal, material counter-
  evidence to "zero variation."
- **The *qiraʾat*:** the canonical readings differ (Ḥafṣ vs. Warsh, etc.);
  the tradition is multiple, not singular.
- **Compositional questions:** academic Qur'anic studies (Neuwirth,
  Sinai, Reynolds) raise editing/arrangement questions for the Qur'an just
  as OT scholarship does for the Pentateuch.

The point is **not** "therefore the Qur'an is corrupt." It is that the
**standard used to convict the Bible, applied evenly, also bites the
Qur'an** — and "an argument that proves too much fails" (Methodology §4).

---

## Arbiter verdict — *tahrif*

**Present.**
- *Muslim case:* the Qur'an accuses some People of the Book of distortion;
  the Bible demonstrably has variants and disputed passages; therefore the
  Bible is unreliable as preserved revelation.
- *Christian / academic reply:* the Qur'an's own affirmation verses and
  the older *tahrif al-maʿna* reading undercut the strong textual charge;
  the Bible's variants are bounded and transparent; and the same
  skepticism applied evenly damages the Qur'an's "no variants" claim.

**Weigh.**
- *Strong:* the Bible is **not letter-perfect** and was humanly
  transmitted/edited — true, and conceded by Christian scholarship.
- *Weak:* "the Bible was wholesale **rewritten/fabricated**" — unsupported
  by manuscript evidence and **not the Qur'an's own most natural claim**.
- *Strong (reverse):* by an even standard, the Qur'an also shows early
  variation (Sanaa) and a multiple reading tradition (*qiraʾat*); the
  "perfectly preserved, zero variants" claim is overstated.
- *Contested:* the precise classical Muslim consensus on *nass* vs.
  *maʿna* — genuinely divided across history.

**Judge.**
- On **interpretive distortion / human transmission** of the Bible: the
  charge is **partially valid** — the Bible is a humanly transmitted,
  variant-bearing, partly composite text. Christianity largely *concedes*
  this and reframes "preservation" as recoverability, not letter-
  perfection.
- On **wholesale textual fabrication** of the Bible: the charge **fails**
  on the manuscript evidence and arguably **fails on the Qur'an's own
  terms**.
- On the **symmetry test**: the strong-form corruption argument, applied
  honestly, **wounds both scriptures' "perfect preservation" claims
  roughly equally**, while leaving **both core messages historically
  recoverable**. The decisive arbiter finding here is therefore: the
  *tahrif* debate is usually won rhetorically by **whoever is allowed to
  apply an asymmetric standard** — and neither side is entitled to that.
