# Chronology 7 — Jesus: Birth, Mission, Crucifixion

Here the two texts are **simultaneously closest and furthest apart**: a
remarkable shared portrait of Jesus' person, broken by one head-on
**historical** contradiction.

## Striking agreement

| | Bible | Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Virgin birth | Matt 1; Luke 1 | Q 19:16–22; 3:47 |
| Title "Messiah" | Yes | *al-Masih* (Q 3:45) |
| Miracles by God's power | Yes | Q 3:49; 5:110 |
| Healed/raised the dead | Yes | Q 3:49 ("by God's leave") |
| Sinless / pure | Yes | Q 19:19 ("a pure boy") |
| Word/Spirit from God | John 1 | Q 4:171 |
| Honoured virgin mother | Luke 1 | Q 19 (whole sura) |
| Will return before the end | Yes | Islamic tradition; Q 43:61 |

No other figure is shared in such high relief. The Qurʾan's nativity is
vivid in its own right: Mary withdraws, the **palm tree and stream**, and
the **infant speaks from the cradle** — "peace be upon me the day I was
born, **the day I die**, and the day I am raised alive" (Q 19:29–33).
*(Note: the clay-bird miracle, Q 3:49/5:110, parallels the apocryphal
**Infancy Gospel of Thomas**, and the cradle-speech the **Arabic Infancy
Gospel** — academic literary observations, recorded neutrally, cutting no
deeper than the Genesis–Gilgamesh parallel in [cap. 12](12-chronology-flood.md).)*

Conversely, the Bible has Jesus make the claims the Qurʾan denies — "I and
the Father are one" (John 10:30), "before Abraham was, **I AM**" (John
8:58), "**It is finished**" at the cross (John 19:30), and the Supper's
self-interpretation "this is my blood… for the forgiveness of sins" (Matt
26:28). The Qurʾanic Jesus **disclaims** exactly this (Q 5:116–117). That
is the [cap. 05](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md) seam and is not re-argued
here.

## The two head-on collisions

### 1. Deity and sonship — already adjudicated
The Qur'an explicitly denies Jesus' deity and divine sonship (Q 4:171;
5:72–75; 19:88–93). This is the [cap. 05](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md) /
[cap. 09](09-nature-of-god.md) seam, not re-litigated here.

### 2. The crucifixion — a factual contradiction

- **Bible:** Jesus was crucified, died, was buried, and rose — the
  **center** of the message, in all four Gospels and the earliest creed
  (1 Cor 15:3–8, datable to within a few years of the events).
- **Qur'an:** "they did **not kill him nor crucify him**, but it was made
  to **appear so** to them… **God raised him to Himself**" (Q 4:157–158).
  Mainstream Muslim reading: a substitution / it only seemed so.

This is the one place the arbiter's **own evidentiary rules** (Methodology
§1: datable physical/historical evidence outranks later tradition) yield a
**clear lean — on the narrow historical question only**:

- The **crucifixion under Pilate** is regarded by secular and critical
  historians (Ehrman, Crossan, Sanders, Lüdemann) as **one of the most
  securely established facts of ancient history**: multiply attested
  (all four Gospels + Paul), **early** (1 Cor 15:3–8 is a creed within a
  few years), **externally** noted by **Tacitus** (*Annals* 15.44) and the
  minimal authentic core of **Josephus**, and meeting the **criterion of
  embarrassment** (a crucified messiah was a scandal no movement would
  invent).
- The Qurʾanic denial appears **~600 years later** with **no earlier
  documentary or historical corroboration**, and its meaning is
  **debated even within Islam**.

So, **strictly as a past event — "was Jesus crucified?"** — the evidence
**strongly favours the Bible's claim**. This is a verdict about the
**event**, *not* about its **salvific meaning**, which is out of remit.

## The Qur'an's own text on Jesus' death is not simple

Symmetry and accuracy require resisting the **popular** slogan "Islam says
Jesus never died." The Qurʾanic data is **internally complex**:

- **Q 4:157–158** — not killed, not crucified; "**it was made to appear so
  (*shubbiha lahum*)**"; God **raised him**.
- **Q 19:33** — Jesus speaks of "**the day I die**".
- **Q 3:55** — "**I will take you (*mutawaffīka*)** and raise you to Me" —
  *tawaffā* normally means *cause to die*.
- **Q 4:159** — the People of the Book "will believe in him **before his
  death**".

Classical and modern Muslim readings therefore **diverge**: substitution
(someone else crucified — the majority classical view), the swoon/no-real-
death view, "raised then dies later (at the second coming)", and the
**Ahmadī** natural-death position. The honest statement is: the Qurʾan
**denies the crucifixion specifically**, but **does not cleanly deny
Jesus' death as such**, and Muslim exegesis is itself **unsettled**. The
contradiction with the Bible is therefore **about the crucifixion as the
mode and meaning of death**, not a simple "died vs. never died".

### Resurrection — flagged, not scored
The **death** is near-certain historically. For the **resurrection**,
even skeptical scholars generally grant a **bedrock**: the disciples
*sincerely believed* they saw the risen Jesus, and the movement exploded
from that conviction. **What explains it** — bodily resurrection (Wright,
Habermas, Licona), visionary/hallucination, cognitive-dissonance, or
legend (Lüdemann, Ehrman) — is **contested historically and
theologically**. The arbiter **presents the range and forces no verdict**
on the resurrection itself (Methodology §5).

## Arbiter verdict — Jesus

**Present.** Extensive shared Christology of the **person** (virgin birth,
Messiah, miracles, sinlessness, return); flat contradiction on **deity**
and on **the crucifixion as event**.

**Weigh.**
- *Strong shared core:* the agreed material is large and specific — not
  vague overlap.
- *Decisive on the event:* by the dossier's **own** neutral evidentiary
  standard, the historicity of the crucifixion is **exceptionally well
  attested**; the denial is **late and uncorroborated**.
- *Out of remit:* the **meaning** of the death (atonement) and the
  **resurrection** as a supernatural claim are **not** scored.

**Judge.** This is the **one chapter where the neutral historical method
produces a clear lean**: on the bare factual question *"was Jesus
crucified?"*, the evidence **favours the Bible** and runs **against**
Q 4:157 — explicitly limited to the **historical event**, and with the
added precision that the Qurʾan's broader text on Jesus' *death* is
**internally complex and exegetically unsettled**, so the firm conflict is
specifically over the **crucifixion**, not over whether Jesus ever died.
On *who Jesus is* (God or prophet) and *what the death achieves*
(atonement or nothing to atone — the [cap. 11](11-chronology-fall.md)
seam), the arbiter rules only that each system is **internally coherent**
and that the **truth of those claims is out of scope**. The seam reaches
its sharpest point: the Fall created the need, and here the texts split on
whether the need was met — and even on whether the defining event
happened.
