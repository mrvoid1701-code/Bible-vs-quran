# The Nature of God in Both Religions

*Part II opens here.* Before tracing the shared storyline (creation →
end), the prior question must be settled: **who/what is God** in each
text? This chapter is built **only from the scriptures themselves**, read
logically, with verse evidence on the table. Tradition is cited only to
show how each community *read* its own text.

> Method (per [Methodology](01-methodology.md)): claims are sourced to
> verses; "the tradition says X" is marked as interpretation, not text;
> every attribute is tested **symmetrically**.

---

## 1. What both texts agree on

Read plainly, the Hebrew Bible, the New Testament, and the Qur'an assert a
**large shared core**:

| Attribute | Bible (sample) | Qur'an (sample) |
|---|---|---|
| One / unique | Deut 6:4; Isa 45:5 | Q 112:1–4; 2:255 |
| Eternal, uncaused | Ps 90:2 | Q 57:3 ("the First and the Last") |
| Creator of all | Gen 1:1 | Q 39:62 |
| Omnipotent | Jer 32:17 | Q 2:20 |
| Omniscient | Ps 139:1–4 | Q 6:59 |
| Sovereign / wills freely | Dan 4:35 | Q 85:16 ("Doer of what He wills") |
| Just | Deut 32:4 | Q 4:40 |
| Merciful | Ps 103:8 | Q 1:1–3 (*al-Rahman al-Rahim*) |
| Transcendent, unlike creation | Isa 55:8–9 | Q 42:11 ("nothing like Him") |

So the dispute is **not** "personal creator vs. not." Both affirm one
transcendent, omnipotent, just, merciful Creator. The real divergences are
sharper and fewer — and they are textual, not vague.

---

## 2. The decisive divergences (text + logic)

### 2.1 Unity: absolutely simple vs. triune

- **Qur'an:** unity is **numerical and indivisible**. *Tawhid* forbids any
  internal plurality; Q 112 ("He neither begets nor is born"), Q 4:171
  ("do not say 'three'"), Q 5:73. *Shirk* (association) is the one
  unforgivable sin (Q 4:48).
- **Bible:** one God (Deut 6:4) **plus** an early high-Christology and
  triadic strand (John 1:1; Matt 28:19; 2 Cor 13:14) → the **Trinity**:
  one essence, three persons.
- *Already adjudicated in* [Monotheism vs. Trinity](05-monotheism-vs-trinity.md):
  the Qur'an states its unity **explicitly**; the Bible's triune unity is
  **synthesized, not stated**; "1=3" as a bare contradiction **fails**,
  but "not plainly biblical, metaphysically heavy" **stands**.

### 2.2 Is God *relational love by nature*?

This is the most underrated textual contrast and deserves the evidence in
full.

- **Bible — love is ontologically prior and intrinsic.**
  - "**God *is* love**" — a statement of essence, not just action
    (1 John 4:8,16).
  - The Father loves the Son **"before the foundation of the world"**
    (John 17:24) — i.e., God is loving *eternally, independent of
    creation*. On the Trinitarian reading this is logically tidy: love
    requires a beloved, and the persons supply it within God Himself.
  - Love is shown to the **undeserving**: Rom 5:8 ("while we were still
    sinners").
- **Qur'an — mercy is foundational; love is real but largely
  conditional.**
  - Mercy is primary and vast: every chapter but one opens with
    *al-Rahman al-Rahim*; "**My mercy encompasses all things**"
    (Q 7:156); "Allah has prescribed mercy upon Himself" (Q 6:12).
  - But the verb "**Allah loves**" is repeatedly **conditioned on the
    recipient's conduct**: He loves the doers of good (Q 2:195), the
    just (Q 5:42), the God-fearing (Q 9:4), the patient (Q 3:146) — and
    the recurring negative: "**Allah does not love** the
    transgressors / the unjust / the arrogant / the corrupters"
    (Q 2:190; 3:57; 4:36; 5:64; 28:77; and others).
  - Strict transcendence (*tanzih*) also resists the idea of God
    *needing* or *being constituted by* relation.

**Logical reading (no theology imported):** on the *plain wording*, the
Bible asserts love as **God's essence, prior to and independent of
creation and merit**; the Qur'an asserts **mercy as foundational** but
**love as a response to the worthy**. Neither is incoherent. But they make
**different ontological claims about God's interior life**, and the texts
say so themselves.

### 2.3 Fatherhood and self-disclosure

- **Bible:** God is **Father** (Matt 6:9), and His ultimate self-
  revelation is **incarnational** — "the Word became flesh" (John 1:14),
  "he who has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). God can be *known*
  by drawing near in a person.
- **Qur'an:** fatherhood/begetting is **explicitly denied** of God
  (Q 19:88–93; 112:3); God reveals **His will via a book and prophets**,
  not by indwelling a human. God is known by His **signs** (*ayat*) and
  **names**, not by incarnation.
- *Logical note:* these are **mutually exclusive at the level of
  mechanism** — "God reveals Himself by becoming man" vs. "God categorically
  does not enter creation." Not an emphasis difference; a contradiction of
  models. Each is internally consistent on its own terms.

### 2.4 Forgiveness: by sovereign decree vs. through atonement

- **Qur'an:** God forgives **directly, by His will**, on repentance —
  "Allah forgives all sins" (Q 39:53); **no mediator or sacrifice is
  required** ("no bearer of burdens bears another's burden", Q 35:18).
- **Bible (NT):** forgiveness is tied to **atonement** — "without the
  shedding of blood there is no forgiveness" (Heb 9:22); the cross
  reconciles justice and mercy (Rom 3:25–26).
- *Logical note:* this is a **structural divergence about how divine
  justice and mercy relate**. Islam: mercy can simply remit, sovereignly.
  Christianity: a holy God's justice must be *satisfied*, and substitution
  satisfies it. Both are coherent systems; they cannot **both** describe
  the same God's actual modus operandi.

### 2.5 Anthropomorphic language and "where" God is

- **Both** texts use bodily/spatial language: Bible — God's "hand,"
  "face," "arm," "throne"; Qur'an — God's "hand" (Q 48:10), "face"
  (Q 55:27), "establishment over the Throne" (*istiwa*, Q 20:5).
- **Both communities split the same way internally:** literalist,
  metaphorical, and "affirm-without-asking-how" camps.
  - Islam: Mu'tazila (figurative) vs. Ash'ari (*bila kayf*, "without
    how") vs. Athari/literal-affirming.
  - Christianity: anthropomorphism as accommodation; the Incarnation as
    the controlled, intended "localization" of God.
- *Symmetry verdict:* the "your scripture is crudely anthropomorphic"
  jab **cuts both ways equally** and is a non-argument unless applied
  evenly (Methodology §3).

### 2.6 Decree, freedom, and evil

- **Qur'an:** strong divine determination — *qadar*; "Allah leaves astray
  whom He wills and guides whom He wills" (Q 14:4); yet humans are
  accountable.
- **Bible:** sovereignty (Eph 1:11) held in tension with human
  responsibility — the very tension that produced the internal
  Calvinist/Arminian debate.
- *Symmetry verdict:* the **problem of reconciling omnipotent decree with
  human freedom is shared**, with parallel internal schools on both sides.
  Neither text "solves" it; neither is uniquely indicted by it.

## 3. Names as evidence

How a text *names* God reveals its theology proper:

- **Qur'an — the 99 Names (*al-asma al-husna*, Q 7:180; 59:22–24):**
  dominated by **sovereignty + mercy**: *al-Rahman, al-Rahim, al-Malik,
  al-Quddus, al-Aziz, al-Jabbar, al-Ghaffar*. **No name "Father."**
- **Hebrew Bible:** *YHWH* (the covenant name, Exod 3:14 "I AM"),
  *El Shaddai*, *YHWH-Yireh*, *Qadosh* (Holy) — **covenantal relationship**
  is built into the naming.
- **New Testament:** **"Father," "Abba"** (Rom 8:15), and divine titles
  applied to the Son (*Kyrios*) — naming carries the relational and
  Trinitarian claim.

The naming sets confirm §2: the Qur'an's God is named for **majesty and
mercy**; the Bible's God is named for **covenant and fatherhood**.

## Arbiter verdict — the nature of God

**Present.**
- *Shared:* one, eternal, omnipotent, omniscient, just, merciful,
  transcendent Creator — a genuinely large common core.
- *Islamic distinctives:* absolute simple unity; mercy foundational;
  forgiveness by sovereign decree; no incarnation; God known by signs,
  names, and revelation.
- *Christian distinctives:* triune unity; **love as God's essence, prior
  to creation**; fatherhood; self-revelation by incarnation; forgiveness
  through atonement.

**Weigh.**
- *Strong & textual:* the Qur'an's God is **more simply and explicitly
  stated** (unity, transcendence) — a real clarity advantage on its own
  text.
- *Strong & textual:* the Bible **explicitly predicates love of God's
  essence and locates it before creation** (1 John 4:8; John 17:24) — the
  Qur'an's own wording instead ties God's *love* (not mercy) to the
  recipient's merit (the repeated "Allah does not love…"). This is **not
  apologetic spin; it is what the verbs do** in each text.
- *Structural, not cosmetic:* incarnation vs. non-incarnation, and
  atonement vs. sovereign remission, are **logically exclusive models** —
  the texts cannot both be describing the same God's actual operation.
- *Symmetric / non-decisive:* anthropomorphism and the decree-vs-freedom
  problem indict **neither uniquely**.

**Judge.**
1. **On the shared core:** essentially a **draw** — both texts describe a
   recognizably overlapping supreme God.
2. **On clarity and parsimony of the God-concept:** advantage **Qur'an** —
   it states unity and transcendence without needing later metaphysical
   scaffolding.
3. **On God as intrinsically relational love:** advantage **Bible** — *on
   the plain text*, love is God's essence and is pre-creational and
   unmerited; the Qur'an's text makes God's *love* conditional while
   making His *mercy* foundational. Whether "simpler" or "relationally
   richer" is the *superior* trait is a value premise the arbiter **cannot
   adjudicate** (Methodology §5).
4. **On forgiveness and self-revelation:** **not a difference of emphasis
   but of mechanism** — the two cannot both be literally true of one God.
   Here the arbiter rules only that **each model is internally
   coherent**; deciding which is *real* is a theological-truth question,
   out of scope.

**Bottom line:** the two Gods share a wide trunk and diverge on a few
**load-bearing** branches — *triunity, intrinsic love, incarnation,
mechanism of forgiveness*. These are **textually demonstrable and mutually
exclusive**, not mere tonal differences. That is exactly the fault line
the chronology (creation → end) will keep re-exposing.
